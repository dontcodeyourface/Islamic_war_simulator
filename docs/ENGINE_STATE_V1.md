
# EngineState v1 — Canonical Runtime State Contract

**Status:** canonical design / pre-code  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** 2026-09-23  
**Depends on:** Snapshot v1, Engine Dynamics v1  
**Implementation:** not started

---

## 1. Purpose

EngineState v1 is the exact contract between the static Snapshot v1 evidence layer and the future simulation engine.

Snapshot v1 answers:

> What do we know about a country?

EngineState v1 answers:

> What compact state does the engine evolve every day?

The contract is country-independent.

The same state definition must work for Spain, Morocco, France, Germany, a future Southeast-Asian country, a Sahel state or any other country that satisfies Snapshot v1.

---

## 2. State classes

EngineState v1 contains four different kinds of state.

### A. Physical stocks

Observed or derived quantities with real units:

- persons;
- days of fuel;
- USD reserves;
- tonnes of gold;
- prison population.

These are not forced into 0–1 normalization.

### B. Country scalar latents

Exactly **60 generic scalar variables**.

Most use the range:

```text
0.0 ... 1.0
```

where direction is defined by metadata.

One variable, `demographic_momentum`, is signed:

```text
-1.0 ... +1.0
```

The 60 variables form the normal L1 country runtime.

### C. Regimes

Discrete classifiers such as:

- national escalation phase;
- security-force regime;
- prison-control regime;
- organized-crime regime.

Regimes are outputs of underlying state.

They must never replace the underlying continuous variables.

### D. Dynamic collections

Collections that are instantiated only when evidence or refinement requires them:

- demographic cohorts;
- extremist networks;
- criminal networks;
- regional/fractal nodes.

They are deliberately not represented as fixed country scalars.

# PART I — PHYSICAL STOCKS

## 3. Canonical physical stocks

The machine-readable registry defines 12 optional or required physical stocks.

### 3.1 Population

```text
population_total
working_age_population
dependent_population
```

`population_total` is mandatory.

The other two may be null until resolved from demographic data.

### 3.2 Displacement and reception

```text
displaced_people
reception_population
```

Default initialization may be zero only when the factual baseline contains no displacement/reception stock.

A nonzero factual baseline must be preserved.

### 3.3 Strategic-resource stocks

```text
fuel_stock_days_equivalent
strategic_food_stock_days
```

Null is permitted where Snapshot v1 lacks a defensible national value.

### 3.4 State coercive stocks

```text
available_police_personnel
available_military_personnel
prison_population
```

These retain real counts where available.

They do not directly equal effective capacity.

For example:

```text
police personnel != police_effective_capacity
```

because readiness, logistics, command cohesion and fatigue matter.

### 3.5 Financial stocks

```text
foreign_reserves_usd
gold_reserves_tonnes
```

They contribute to `financial_buffer` but remain separately auditable.

# PART II — THE 60 COUNTRY LATENTS

## 4. Demographics and social state — 8 variables

### 4.1 demographic_momentum

Range:

```text
-1 ... +1
```

Meaning:

- negative: structural population decline;
- zero: approximate demographic equilibrium;
- positive: structural population growth.

Inputs may include:

- total fertility;
- age structure;
- natural increase;
- migration;
- religious switching where measured;
- cohort composition.

This is a slow variable.

It is not a conflict variable.

### 4.2 migration_pressure

Range:

```text
0 ... 1
```

Represents migration inflow relative to economic, housing, administrative and integration absorption capacity.

It is not equivalent to absolute immigrant population.

### 4.3 dependency_pressure

Represents fiscal/social pressure from dependent population relative to working-age capacity.

### 4.4 social_cohesion

Higher means greater capacity for different groups/institutions to coexist and cooperate despite stress.

It is conceptually distinct from government legitimacy.

### 4.5 polarization

Higher means more severe political/social polarization.

### 4.6 communal_fear

Captures short-run fear between identity communities after violence, perceived threat or inflammatory information.

This is one of the principal inputs to reciprocal radicalization.

### 4.7 reception_pressure

Demand on asylum/humanitarian/social reception capacity relative to effective capacity.

### 4.8 displacement_pressure

Pressure generated by internally or externally displaced populations.

---

## 5. Institutions and governance — 10 variables

### 5.1 government_legitimacy

Public/institutional acceptance of the current governing authority.

### 5.2 state_cohesion

Overall coherence of the state apparatus.

This is broader than government popularity.

### 5.3 elite_cohesion

Degree to which political, administrative, economic and security elites continue to accept the same constitutional/order framework.

### 5.4 administrative_capacity

Ability to continue ordinary state administration.

### 5.5 judicial_continuity

Ability of courts, prosecutors and related institutions to operate.

### 5.6 fiscal_capacity

Ability to collect revenue, borrow, fund services and sustain crisis expenditure.

### 5.7 salary_payment_continuity

Ability to continue paying state employees and security personnel.

This is kept separate because state payroll failure can destabilize command structures faster than aggregate GDP collapse.

### 5.8 center_region_cohesion

Institutional cohesion between central and subnational authorities.

Especially relevant to devolved/federal countries.

### 5.9 territorial_control

Share/quality of effective state administrative-security control over national territory.

This is a high-value phase-transition variable.

### 5.10 regional_fragmentation_pressure

Pressure toward subnational institutional separation, competing authority or local autonomy under crisis.

---

## 6. Security and internal order — 9 variables

### 6.1 police_effective_capacity

Effective, deployable policing/public-order capability.

Headcount is only one contributor.

### 6.2 intelligence_effectiveness

Ability to detect, attribute, anticipate and disrupt relevant security threats.

### 6.3 security_chain_cohesion

Coherence of police, gendarmerie, security-service and related internal-security chains.

### 6.4 command_cohesion

Operational unity of command across coercive institutions.

### 6.5 constitutional_loyalty

Institutional adherence to the recognized legal/constitutional command structure.

This is not inferred from voting preferences.

### 6.6 operational_fatigue

Accumulated exhaustion from sustained deployment, casualties, overtime, stress and insufficient recovery.

Higher is worse.

### 6.7 public_order_stress

Current demand placed on public-order/security services.

### 6.8 prison_control

Effective control of the prison system.

Higher is better.

### 6.9 terrorism_threat_pressure

Current aggregate terrorism/extremist-violence threat pressure after semantic normalization.

It must not be computed by naïvely dividing national alert-level numbers.

---

## 7. Non-state coercive networks — 8 variables

### 7.1 criminal_network_power

Aggregate capacity of material organized-crime networks.

### 7.2 black_market_expansion

Degree to which scarcity, disorder or state weakness has expanded illicit market relevance.

### 7.3 violent_islamist_pressure

Pressure generated specifically by violent Islamist networks.

It is analytically separate from Muslim population share.

### 7.4 violent_ultranationalist_pressure

Pressure generated by violent ultranationalist networks.

It is separate from ordinary conservative/nationalist political populations.

### 7.5 revolutionary_extremist_pressure

Pressure generated by violent revolutionary networks.

It is separate from ordinary left-wing populations.

### 7.6 organized_armed_actor_capacity

Aggregate non-state capacity for sustained organized armed political violence.

This is intentionally generic and may receive contributions from different explicit network collections.

### 7.7 foreign_support_nonstate

External financial, organizational, political or material support available to non-state violent actors.

Stored only at strategic aggregate level.

### 7.8 civilizational_narrative_strength

Degree to which local conflict has been reframed successfully as a wider transnational/civilizational struggle.

This is central to the README S6 logic.

---

## 8. Economy, resources and infrastructure — 13 variables

### 8.1 economic_output

Normalized productive/economic functionality relative to baseline potential.

### 8.2 inflation_pressure

Higher means stronger inflationary stress.

### 8.3 unemployment_pressure

Higher means stronger labor-market stress.

### 8.4 financial_buffer

Capacity to absorb crisis through:

- reserves;
- borrowing;
- fiscal room;
- gold/FX buffer;
- external financial support.

### 8.5 trade_access

Effective access to international trade flows relevant to sustaining the economy.

### 8.6 energy_resilience

Strategic resilience of the national energy system.

### 8.7 electricity_functionality

Current functionality of electricity generation, transmission and distribution.

### 8.8 fuel_distribution_functionality

Ability to distribute available petroleum products to the economy/state.

Fuel stock and distribution are distinct.

### 8.9 food_resilience

Ability to maintain adequate food supply.

### 8.10 water_resilience

Ability to sustain water availability and distribution.

### 8.11 transport_logistics_functionality

Aggregate road/rail/port/airport logistics functionality.

### 8.12 telecom_internet_functionality

Aggregate communications functionality.

### 8.13 health_system_functionality

Ability of the health system to continue operating under stress.

---

## 9. Military and technology — 10 variables

### 9.1 military_land_effective

Effective land-domain capability.

### 9.2 military_air_effective

Effective air-domain capability.

### 9.3 military_naval_effective

Effective naval-domain capability.

### 9.4 military_isr_ew_effective

Combined strategic-level ISR/C4ISR/EW effectiveness.

### 9.5 military_drone_cuas_effective

Effective drone and counter-UAS maturity/capacity.

### 9.6 military_logistics_availability

Fraction/quality of military capability actually supportable by fuel, transport, maintenance, personnel and supplies.

### 9.7 defence_industrial_capacity

Underlying domestic capacity to manufacture/repair relevant defence materiel.

### 9.8 replenishment_capacity

Ability to replace losses through:

- domestic production;
- repair;
- allied supply;
- imports;
- mobilization.

### 9.9 ai_defence_maturity

Maturity of defence-relevant AI adoption.

### 9.10 cyber_resilience

Ability of state/military/critical systems to continue functioning under cyber pressure.

---

## 10. External-system state — 2 variables

### 10.1 alliance_support

Effective support available from alliance/partner systems at the current moment.

This is dynamic.

Treaty membership alone does not imply `1.0`.

### 10.2 external_conflict_pressure

Current pressure generated by interstate confrontation and hostile external actors.

This can rise before formal war.

## 11. Count check

The canonical scalar count is:

| Block | Count |
|---|---:|
| Demographics/social | 8 |
| Institutions/governance | 10 |
| Security | 9 |
| Non-state | 8 |
| Economy/resources | 13 |
| Military/technology | 10 |
| External system | 2 |
| **Total** | **60** |

No additional country scalar may be added casually after this contract.

If a new effect can be represented by:

- a physical stock;
- a dynamic collection;
- an event;
- a graph edge;
- an existing latent;

it should not become a new scalar.

A 61st scalar requires explicit EngineState schema review.

# PART IV — DYNAMIC COLLECTIONS

## 16. Demographic cohorts

Demographic detail must use a keyed collection rather than adding permanent scalar fields.

Possible dimensions include:

- religion;
- age band;
- generation;
- nationality/origin where directly measured.

Example conceptual key:

```text
cohort:
  id
  semantic_category
  population
  fertility
  mortality
  migration
  age_distribution
```

The engine must preserve the original semantic category.

Foreign nationality cannot silently become Muslim identity.

---

## 17. Extremist networks

Each material network may become an explicit object.

Allowed high-level families:

```text
violent_islamist
violent_ultranationalist
revolutionary
separatist
```

Example state:

```text
network_id
family
parent_node
size_proxy
organization
financing
propaganda
external_support
weapons_access
counterterrorism_pressure
community_rejection
```

Country-level pressure variables aggregate these network objects.

---

## 18. Criminal networks

Explicit criminal actors are created only if material.

State may include:

```text
network_id
markets
financial_capacity
logistics
territorial_influence
violence_capacity
weapons_access
prison_links
international_links
relationship_to_conflict_actors
```

Individual leadership can be represented at this level if a future scenario genuinely requires it.

It is not a country-level scalar.

---

## 19. Regional/fractal nodes

Regional nodes are created from:

- explicit regional data; or
- adaptive refinement.

They use the same core state contract where appropriate.

Every node must preserve parent lineage and aggregation weights.

# PART V — INITIALIZATION FROM SNAPSHOT V1

## 20. Initialization philosophy

There must be no hidden default country.

A country starts only when every required runtime latent has one of:

1. direct initialization from evidence;
2. composite initialization from evidence;
3. explicit model prior with uncertainty;
4. explicit scenario override.

The following is forbidden:

```text
missing value -> silently use 0.5
```

---

## 21. Initialization rule IDs

Every scalar has an `init_rule_id`.

Examples:

```text
DEM_MOMENTUM_INIT_V1
STATE_COHESION_INIT_V1
POLICE_CAPACITY_INIT_V1
ENERGY_RESILIENCE_INIT_V1
MIL_LAND_INIT_V1
ALLIANCE_SUPPORT_INIT_V1
```

The machine-readable registry is:

`config/engine/engine_state_v1.yaml`

---

## 22. Direct initialization

Use when Snapshot v1 already expresses the engine concept closely.

Example:

```text
snapshot:
  security_force_cohesion.command_cohesion

        ↓ semantic normalization

engine:
  command_cohesion
```

Direct does not mean source units must already be 0–1.

The normalization step is still versioned.

---

## 23. Composite initialization

Many state variables combine multiple Snapshot fields.

Example conceptual energy initialization:

```text
energy_resilience =
f(
  strategic_petroleum_stock_days,
  import_dependency,
  domestic_refining,
  electricity_diversification,
  storage,
  external_supply_access
)
```

No weights are frozen yet.

The future normalization contract must define them separately.

---

## 24. Prior-based initialization

If required data are missing, the model may use a prior only when:

- the prior is explicit;
- the rationale is documented;
- uncertainty bounds exist;
- the sampled value is recorded;
- the RNG stream is `uncertainty_sampling`.

Example:

```text
Snapshot field = null
        ↓
MODEL_PRIOR_X_V1
        ↓
distribution(parameters)
        ↓
sampled initial latent
        ↓
uncertainty trace
```

---

## 25. Scenario override

Scenario packs may override initial state only explicitly.

Every override records:

- scenario ID;
- variable;
- baseline initial value;
- overridden value;
- rationale;
- provenance class.

No scenario may silently mutate the Snapshot file itself.

# PART VI — OWNERSHIP AND WRITE RULES

## 26. Single module owner

Every scalar has one primary module owner.

Examples:

```text
government_legitimacy -> institutions
police_effective_capacity -> security
economic_output -> economy_resources
military_land_effective -> military
violent_islamist_pressure -> non_state_networks
```

Other modules submit pressure/contribution inputs.

They should not directly overwrite the variable.

---

## 27. Contribution model

Preferred pattern:

```text
module A contribution
module B contribution
event contribution
spillover contribution
        ↓
owner equation
        ↓
raw delta
        ↓
constraints
        ↓
new value
```

This reduces hidden cross-module side effects.

---

## 28. Multiple contributors

A variable may have many contributors.

Example `government_legitimacy`:

- economic-output change;
- inflation;
- security failures;
- service continuity;
- information credibility;
- casualties/events;
- successful crisis response.

But only the institutions module resolves them into the final daily delta.

# PART VII — CONSTRAINTS AND CONSERVATION

## 29. Normalized bounds

Most latents use:

```text
0 <= x <= 1
```

Every clamp must be traced.

### demographic_momentum

Uses:

```text
-1 <= x <= 1
```

---

## 30. Physical conservation

Where physical stocks are modeled, updates must conserve quantities or record the source/sink.

Example:

```text
fuel[t+1] =
fuel[t]
+ imports
+ production
- civilian use
- military use
- losses
```

No resource may appear from an untracked source.

---

## 31. Population accounting

Where demographic cohorts are enabled:

```text
sum(cohorts) ~= population_total
```

within defined tolerance.

Overlapping categories must not be summed as if mutually exclusive.

For example:

- religion;
- nationality;
- generation;

may overlap and therefore require separate cohort dimensions.


---

## 51. State hashes

The engine should compute a deterministic hash of the canonical state representation:

- before each daily update;
- after each daily update.

This allows:

- replay validation;
- divergence localization;
- corruption detection.

---

## 52. Trace depth

The trace system must support at least three levels:

### Level 1 — human summary

Why did the phase change?

### Level 2 — causal variables/events

Which variables and events caused it?

### Level 3 — technical provenance

Which equations, parameters, raw Snapshot fields and source IDs contributed?

# PART XIII — MISSING DATA POLICY

## 55. Required versus optional

All 60 latents are required at runtime.

This does **not** mean all Snapshot fields must be populated.

A missing empirical contributor may be handled through a documented prior.

Physical stocks may remain null if the dynamic equation can work through normalized latent state without fabricating a physical quantity.

---

## 56. No anonymous 0.5

The engine must reject initialization such as:

```text
if missing:
    x = 0.5
```

unless `0.5` is explicitly defined by a versioned prior with rationale and uncertainty.

---

## 57. Confidence metadata

Every initial latent should carry:

```text
data_quality
uncertainty_width
empirical_fraction
prior_fraction
freshness
```

These metadata do not necessarily evolve as simulation variables but remain attached to trace/provenance.

# PART XV — PRE-IMPLEMENTATION GATE

## 59. What is still intentionally undefined

EngineState v1 fixes the **variables**, not yet the numerical formulas.

Still to define before engine implementation:

1. normalization equations;
2. parameter registry;
3. baseline priors for missing data;
4. correlation groups;
5. graph-edge normalization;
6. phase thresholds;
7. memory/decay constants;
8. random-shock hazard families.

These belong in the next contract:

`NORMALIZATION_AND_PARAMETERS_V1.md`

---

## 60. What must not change casually

Before implementation, any proposal to add another permanent country scalar must answer:

1. Why can it not be represented by an existing latent?
2. Why can it not be a physical stock?
3. Why can it not be a dynamic collection?
4. Why can it not be an event?
5. Why can it not be a graph edge?
6. Why can it not be a derived view?

If any existing mechanism is sufficient, the new scalar should not be added.

---

## 61. Canonical runtime principle

The country runtime should remain small enough to understand.

The data layer can be rich.

The event layer can be rich.

The fractal layer can become detailed when required.

But the ordinary country state remains:

> **12 physical stock fields + 60 generic latents + 4 regimes + dynamic collections.**

This is the EngineState v1 contract.
